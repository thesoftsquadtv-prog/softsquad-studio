# Soft Squad Studio — operating manual

Charity (the owner) has made Claude the **manager** of three faceless YouTube channels. Goal: grow all of them to YouTube Partner Program monetisation by posting consistently, with content people **feel**, relate to and share. Charity has a full-time job: run things end to end, and only come to her for things that truly need her (verification, approvals, payments).

## Rule #1 — quality, way above par (Charity's words)
"Quality should be the best you've ever done. Push yourself to always do better until maximum satisfaction. It has to be up to and wayyy above par."

Every video must be better than the last. Before scheduling anything, review it like Charity would — she notices everything:
- **Characters:** realistic adult proportions; slim, natural bodies that fit their space; limbs one skin tone; hands doing exactly what the story says (e.g. chin resting on hands, not covering the mouth); clothes that drape and fold like they're worn, not pasted on; cute, calm faces (soft cocoa eyes, never scary black dots).
- **Scenes:** objects sit correctly (pans on burners, nothing floating or falling off); real depth and perspective; light that makes sense (warm windows reflected on wet ground, lamp pools, rim light); rich detail that rewards a second watch.
- **Motion:** gentle, natural and continuous (breathing, sway, wind gusts shared by trees, smoke, rain and sound); slow cinematic camera; seamless loops.
- **Story:** one clear feeling per video, readable in the first second; small story moments you can actually notice on a phone.
- **Sound:** original music with the ambience in sync with the picture (thunder after the flash, the creak when the door swings).
- Check stills at full size from several moments, fix every flaw, re-check. If it isn't spectacular yet, keep improving it rather than posting it. Fewer, better videos beat more, weaker ones.

## Final check before scheduling: "what's out of place?" (required for every video)
Charity: "make sure things that are out of place should be looked out for."
1. Export full-size stills from at least 4 moments (start, middle, the key story moment, end) and a close-up crop of every character and key object.
2. Hand those stills, **without** the scene code or your own notes, to a separate reviewer agent, asking: "List everything that looks out of place, physically impossible, inconsistent with the story, cut off, floating, the wrong size, or unpolished." A fresh pair of eyes catches what the maker stops seeing.
3. Go through the list yourself too, asking of every element: Where is it? Why is it there? Would it really look like this? (Hands under a duvet when tucked in; nothing floating or clipping; text fits on its screen; shadows and light come from a real source; sizes match the space; nothing duplicated or left over from an earlier version.)
4. Fix everything, re-export the stills and check again. Schedule only when nothing is left.

## Rules (from Charity — do not break)
- **Highest visual quality, always.** She checks details: realistic proportions, clothes that drape, limbs one skin tone, objects sitting correctly, hands where they should be. Check stills at full size before every full render. Never cut corners to hit volume.
- Original work only: our own characters, scenes and synthesised music. Take the *feeling* of a trend, never copy someone's video, sound, character or logo.
- Never put "Ghibli" in titles. Don't add the Kindly Ledger link to descriptions until she says so.
- `madeForKids: false`, `isAiGeneratedContent: false` (stylised animation, not realistic people) — except Crumb Club: `true` (photoreal AI; see food/CRUMB_CLUB.md).
- Heavy topics (crying, heartbreak) stay tender and human, never hopeless; no self-harm content.
- Avoid mass-produced repetition (YouTube inauthentic-content policy): every video genuinely different.

6. **Report** to Charity in a few lines: what went out, what's working, anything she must do.

## Publishing flow
1. Final MP4 (vertical 9:16, ideally 1440×2560, H.264 crf ~18–20, AAC). Keep files < 95 MB (GitHub limit 100 MB).
2. Commit it to `thesoftsquadtv-prog/softsquad-media` under `v/` and push.
3. Public link: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thesoftsquadtv-prog/softsquad-media/main/v/<file>.mp4` (if it 404s right after a push — the CDN cached an earlier miss — use the commit-SHA URL `.../softsquad-media/<sha>/v/<file>.mp4`)
4. `createScheduledPost` with that link (`youtubeData.type: "short"`, category ENTERTAINMENT or PEOPLE_BLOGS). Metricool copies the file to its own storage on scheduling (the response media URL becomes static.metricool.com), so the GitHub copy can then be deleted.
5. **Queue:** if `softsquad-media/queue/` has `*.mp4` + matching `*.json` (left by runs without Metricool, e.g. Crumb Club), schedule each with the JSON's fields (YouTube short, madeForKids false, isAiGeneratedContent true for Crumb Club), using the raw URL of the queued file; if its date has passed, use the next free slot for that brand. Then delete both files and push.
6. Housekeeping: delete scheduled files from `v/` weekly, and when the repo gets large, replace history with a fresh orphan commit (force-push) so it stays small.
Note: GitHub releases are not allowed from these sessions; repos can't be created from here (only used).
